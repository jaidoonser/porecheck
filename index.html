    How the checking works

    Each ingredient is matched against a list of over 400 known pore-clogging (comedogenic) ingredients and their other names, such as Cocos Nucifera Oil for coconut oil or the CI numbers for red dyes. Seaweeds and algae are matched by their plant family, so any extract of them is caught. The list was last brought into line with the stricter clinic pore-clogger lists in October 2026.

    High means a comedogenic rating of 4–5, or an ingredient that nearly every pore-clogger list flags. Moderate means a rating of 2–3: fine for many people, but worth avoiding if your skin is acne-prone.

    Scan ingredients reads the photo on your phone, nothing is uploaded. Small or curved print can come out a bit wrong, so glance over the text it fills in. If it struggles, your camera can copy text straight from a label (Live Text on iPhone, Google Lens on Android) and you can paste it in.

    Scan front of box reads the barcode (or the product name) on your phone and looks it up in Open Beauty Facts, a free public database of beauty products. Not every product is in it yet. When one isn't, a Google search button appears so you can copy the list from a shop's website.

    This is a first check, not medical advice. Different clinics keep slightly different lists.
